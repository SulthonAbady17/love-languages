Relationship
Project

A modern relationship lifestyle brand for Indonesian couples — calm, warm, premium, and intentional. Not dating. Not therapy. Not weddings.

01 — Logo concepts

Five directions

Each concept stands alone as a usable primary mark. All share the same restrained, non-literal approach — no hearts, no rings, no romantic clichés. The connection between two people is suggested through form and space, never spelled out.

Concept 01

Typography only

Set entirely in lowercase Plus Jakarta Sans with wide tracking, stacked in two calm lines. The single terracotta period is the only accent — quiet, confident, and easy to license across sizes without a symbol to protect.

Concept 02

RP monogram

An interlocked R and P share a single stroke weight, ringed by a thin sage circle — a signet-like mark that reads as premium and personal. Works reversed on navy or as a single flat colour at favicon size.

Concept 03

Two connected dots

Two people, drawn as simple weighted dots, joined by one unhurried curve rather than a straight line — growth, not a transaction. The most flexible mark for animation: the curve can gently breathe in-app.

Concept 04

Abstract connection symbol

Two ribbon strokes cross once and separate again — two individual paths that meet, without forming a closed loop or heart. Reads as woven, not tangled. The strongest candidate for an app icon glyph.

Concept 05

Editorial wordmark

A serif italic for "Relationship" carries warmth and voice, set against a tracked-out uppercase sans "Project" for structure — the same tension found in Kinfolk or Cereal magazine mastheads. A thin terracotta rule separates the two registers. Best suited to editorial content, packaging, and print.

02 — Color

Usage

Midnight navy carries authority and calm; sage signals growth and softness; terracotta is used sparingly as the single warm accent — never as a dominant field. Warm off-white replaces pure white everywhere.

03 — Typography

System

Plus Jakarta Sans is the workhorse — UI, body copy, product. A serif italic (shown here as Newsreader; General Sans or Satoshi remain the approved sans alternates) is reserved for editorial moments only, never for interface text.
